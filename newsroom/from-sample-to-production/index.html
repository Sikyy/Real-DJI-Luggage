The approved sample is the specification

In luggage production the approved sample is the reference document. Once you sign it off, it defines the shell, the components, the lining, the branding position and the pack. Bulk production is then checked against it rather than against a written description.

What gets locked at approval

  • Shell material, structure and finish, including the frame on aluminium-frame cases.
  • Wheel and telescopic handle type, and their mounting.
  • Lock or zipperless closure, and the lining layout.
  • Logo position, method and size.
  • Carton, insert and packing configuration.

What can still change after approval

Quantity per size and per colour can change within the agreed structure. Packaging marks and carton labelling can change. What cannot change without a new sample is the structure itself, because tooling and line setup follow the approved build.

Why the handoff matters

Most production disputes trace back to a sample that was approved verbally or with open points. Recording the approved sample, and keeping a signed copy, is the cheapest protection a buyer has. Our bulk window runs 25–55 days from deposit and approved sample, and that clock starts from a closed sample, not an open one.

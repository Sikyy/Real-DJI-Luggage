Material Choices For Hard Shell Luggage

Introduction

Practical notes from DJI Luggage on building reliable suitcase and travel product programs, from early product planning to repeatable production.

Date9.17.25
AuthorDJI Luggage Team
TypeInsights

The hard-shell routes we produce

Hard-shell luggage is not one material. Four routes cover almost every programme we quote, and the right one depends on how the case will be sold and what it has to survive.

Polycarbonate and ABS+PC

PC shells resist cracking and hold shape across a wide temperature range. ABS+PC blends cost less and remain the standard for volume ranges. Both are moulded on site.

PP

Polypropylene is lighter and cheaper, with a softer surface finish. It suits value-focused ranges and large checked sizes where weight matters more than rigidity.

Aluminium frame

An aluminium-frame case uses a PC or ABS+PC shell with an extruded aluminium profile frame, bent and riveted closed rather than welded. The aluminium is the structural frame, not the whole case. The case mouth is tested by Brinell hardness to GB/T 231.1 and must reach at least 40 HWB, as required by QB/T 2155-2018. Anodising is the standard finish, with an oxide film of 10–12 micrometres.

How to choose between them

Ask what the case has to survive and what it has to sell for. Temperature range, airline handling and price band usually decide the material before anything else does. All four routes run at the same 200-unit minimum and the same 25–55 day lead time.
